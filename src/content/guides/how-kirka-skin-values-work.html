Some Kirka.io skins are worth nothing to trade, and a few are valued in the billions of coins. This guide explains what decides a skin's value, what the Hub Value on Kirka Hub means, and how to judge a skin before you trade for it.

What a Hub Value is

Every skin on the Kirka Hub price list has a Hub Value: the price the Kirka Hub valuation index gives it, in Kirka coins. It is based on what the skin actually trades for between players, not on what the game sells it for. When trades for a skin move up or down, its Hub Value is updated, and the change is logged.

A skin listed as TBD has not traded often enough to give it a fair value yet. That usually means it is new, very rare, or nobody is trading it.

A player's inventory value on their profile is simply the Hub Values of everything they own added together, counting each copy.

What makes a skin valuable

How many exist

Kirka shows how many players own each skin, and that number matters more than anything else. The two default character skins are owned by over three million players each. At the other end, dozens of skins are owned by fewer than twenty players. When only a handful of copies exist, anyone who wants one has to convince one of those few owners to give it up.

Whether you can still get it

Skins come into the game in different ways:

  • Chest drops: the most common route. A skin in a chest anyone can open keeps being created, which keeps its supply growing.
  • Seasonal chests, such as the Christmas and Halloween chests, are only around for part of the year.
  • Store purchases with diamonds, often in themed bundles.
  • Rewards, such as watch-time rewards, event quests and ranked season rewards.
  • Default items that every player starts with.

A skin that can no longer be obtained, like an old event or season reward, can only change hands through trading, so its supply is fixed. Those skins tend to hold or grow in value over time.

Rarity

The rarity tier (Common, Rare, Epic, Legendary, Mythical and Paranormal) tells you roughly how hard a skin was to get when it came out. It is a useful first hint, but not a price: an old Legendary that is no longer obtainable can be worth far more than a new Mythical that drops from every chest.

The weapon and demand

Skins for weapons people use all the time, especially the VITA, tend to be in higher demand than skins for weapons fewer people pick. Several of the most valuable skins in the game are VITA skins. Looks matter too: a skin the community thinks is clean or iconic keeps buyers interested.

How to judge a skin before you trade

  1. Check its Hub Value on the price list. Open the skin to see its rarity, how many players own it and how to get it.
  2. Look at recent trades for the same skin in the trade portal. Several trades tell you far more than one.
  3. Check whether it is still obtainable. A skin still dropping from chests is likely to lose value as more copies appear.
  4. Be careful with TBD skins. Without trades there is no fair price, and that is where people get talked into bad deals.

Values are a guide, not a guarantee. Two players can agree on any trade they like; the Hub Value just helps you know whether you are winning or losing it. For more on staying safe, read how to trade safely in Kirka.
