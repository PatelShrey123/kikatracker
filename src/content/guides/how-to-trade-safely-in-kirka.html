Trading is one of the best parts of Kirka.io, and also where players lose their best skins. Almost every bad trade comes from one of a few situations. This guide covers how to check a deal before you accept it and how to spot the common scams.

Before every trade

  1. Price both sides. Look up every skin on the Kirka Hub price list and add up each side. If the other player insists their skin is worth much more than its Hub Value, ask why.
  2. Check recent trades. The trade portal shows completed trades with every skin valued. If the same skin has gone for much less recently, you know where the real price is.
  3. Read the skin's page. How many players own it and whether it is still obtainable tell you whether its value is likely to hold.
  4. Check who you are trading with. Look the player up by their short ID. An account made last week with a huge offer deserves more caution than a long-time player.
  5. Take your time. A good trade will still be there in ten minutes.

Common scams

"You go first"

The other player asks you to give your skin now and promises to send theirs after. Once you have sent it, they disappear. Only trade through the game's own trade system, where both sides are swapped at the same time, and never hand items over on a promise.

Fake middlemen

For big trades, a scammer suggests a "trusted middleman" to hold both items. The middleman is their friend or their second account. Kirka's trade system already swaps both sides at once, so a middleman is not needed.

Value lies

"This skin is really worth 500 million, the list is outdated." Sometimes values do move faster than the list, but this is also the most common way people get talked into a bad deal. Check recent trades yourself instead of taking their word.

The last-second swap

The other player puts the agreed skin in the trade, then quickly swaps it for a similar-looking cheaper one, or removes an item just before you accept. Always read the final trade window carefully before you confirm.

Fake websites and "free skin" links

Links that promise free skins, ask you to log in to "verify" your inventory, or ask for your password are attempts to steal your account. Kirka Hub never asks for your password: to link your account with the Discord bot you only type a short code into the game's lobby chat.

Real money deals

Selling or buying skins for real money outside the game has no protection at all. If the other person does not pay or charges back, there is nothing anyone can do, and it can break the game's rules.

If something feels wrong

  • Pressure, hurry and "only for the next five minutes" are warning signs on their own.
  • An offer far above value is usually bait for one of the scams above.
  • If you are unsure, ask in a community you trust before you accept, and share the trade, not your account.
